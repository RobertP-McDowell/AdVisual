--- About --------------------------------------------------------------------------------
AdVisual is an AdLib/OPL2 Chiptune DAW.

AdVisual has two main panels where you will do your work. The one you start off in is the
Composer, here you will Create and edit the Tracks notes and events.

The Insmaker panel allows you to create and edit instruments, and manage your bnk files.
Click the saxophone icon in the toolbar to switch to it.

You may find the source code for this project at https://github.com/RobertP-McDowell/AdVisual
Read COMPILING.txt to find out how to compile your own modified version of AdVisual!

--- Files --------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The .rol file format is the only song type currently supported, it contains the tracks note
and event info. You can load one of the sample rol files, or start editing from scratch.

The .bnk file format contains Instrument data, which can be edited in the Insmaker panel.

When loading and saving to dialogs will appear, one looking for track files and one for banks,
you can simply press cancel on one of them to load only a track or bank file.

--- Composer -----------------------------------------------------------------------------
---- The Grid:
The grid is made up one dashed vertical line for every beat, and one solid vertical line
for every measure, you may change the frequency of ticks and beats in track options in the
toolbar. Every dashed horizontal line represents a sharp note, if a note isn't directly
on the line then it is a flat note. You can also use the piano guide on the left side as
a more familiar reference; click on the piano guide to sample the sound of a note.

Left click to create a note, hold it to change the length. You can enable audio-feedback
in the toolbar to hear what new notes will sound like. Shift+Release to erase notes.

Right click to place the cursor, hold it to select a range of notes, backspace to delete.
Press Ctrl + X, C, V, to cut, copy and paste selection respectively.

Press Ctrl+Z to undo your last action, Ctrl+Shift+Z to redo your last undo.

---- Events:
Editing the event header, which is placed between the toolbar and the grid, allows you to
change the track tempo multiplier, instrument, pitch precision, and volume multiplier
dynamically during playback. Pitch precision is for advanced usage,

---- Toolbar:
Notice the row of colorful radio buttons, each representing a different channel, click one
to change the channel, Shift+Left Click to enable/disable a channel during playback.

Next is a set of 4 small buttons, the top left eye button will show a preview of notes
for every channel. The top right icon will toggle audio feedback on every new note placed.
The bottom left button will toggle insert/overwrite mode;
This affects most action on the grid that utilize any selected portion, including if
deleting the selection should just erase notes, or all also erase the gap leftover. It also
affects moving, pasting and more.

Last on the toolbar is Track settings, you may set tempo (beats per minute), percussion and more.

---- Percussion:
The OPL2 can support up to 7 melodic channels that use 2 Envelopes, and 4
percussion channels that use one Envelope. Or you can disable percussion entirely in
track settings and get 9 melodic channels as a tradeoff.
It should be noted how the Tom Drum (TD) channel influences the other percussion channels.
The Bass Drum channel is unaffected by the TD channel.
The Tom Drum channel will use its own notes pitch as normal.
The Cymbal channel will use the same pitch as the last TD note.
The Snare Drum and Hi-Hat channels pitch will always be 7 semitones higher than the last TD note.

--- Insmaker -----------------------------------------------------------------------------
Select the instrument you want to edit in the text field in the toolbar, click the
three dots on the right to create/delete an instrument. Click the magnifying glass on the
left to search.

---- Instruments:
Instrument wide settings can be edited by clicking the three dots in the text field.

Melodic instruments use two operators, the Carrier and the Modulator, whereas percussion
instruments just use one; the Modulator (see "Composer->Percussion" section).

You can also toggle Additive synthesis in instrument settings, switching between AM and FM.
Additive synth (Amplitude modulation, or AM), Frequency modulation (FM).
FM is the default and is usually preffered.

---- FM / AM:
An FM instrument's frequency of the carrier is controlled by the modulator,
meaning the modulator changes the pitch of the carrier.
An AM instrument adds the output of the carrier and modulator together,
in other words adding the amplitude (volume) together.

---- Envelopes:
Envelope is a term that applies to both the Carrier and the Modulator.
Both envelopes have almost the same set of properties. mess around for a little and see what
sounds good, you can use the arrow keys to navigate if you prefer.

Use the piano guide below to sample the instrument.

Unsaved envelope properties are represented by an asterisk(*) next to them.
